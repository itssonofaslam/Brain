# Research checklist (applies to EVERY reference; set by Salman 2026-10-26; overrides earlier cut/animation decisions when 2nd reference differs)
Rule: I (Claude) self-interrogate. He knows little of the technical side. Hex codes for every colour. Save notes after every step (usage-limit proof). Ask him for the full video when needed.
Per reference, log WITH timestamps:
1. Camera: angle changes, closeups, wides, pushes/pans, 2D->3D camera motion (parallax/true 3D?), where cinematic vs explainer mode.
2. Explainer mode: board/chalkboard/diagram appears + animations on it, intro-drawing style segments.
3. Effects: which, where, duration, why (psychology).
4. 3D illusion: how achieved; shadows (type, direction, softness, contact shadows, colour), lighting (sources, rim, glow from doors/windows, light cones, highlights on lit objects), depth cues.
5. Blur: where, type (gaussian, bokeh, motion, depth-of-field), focus pulls/rack focus.
6. Palette in hex per scene/mood; day vs night; grade.
7. Text: where it appears, animated or not, style, why.
8. Letterbox bars: exact position/size (match exactly: not more, not less). Reference 1: 41 px top + 41 px bottom of 720.
9. Characters: shapes, outfits, outfit changes, ageing, variants, how brain invents new ones.
10. Environments: how built, objects in them (birds, props, ambient life), what makes it feel alive, how brain thinks/invents (e.g. glow from a door in daylight).
11. Physics: weight, gravity, bounce, overshoot, inertia, cloth/hair, secondary motion.
12. Transitions: every type, placement.
13. Script+video structure: suspense placement, emotion per beat, pacing, hooks, payoffs.
14. Code style the brain must follow exactly; what is needed to reach the full look; no quality compromise.
15. What makes it premium / what an animation NEEDS / what makes it cinematic.
