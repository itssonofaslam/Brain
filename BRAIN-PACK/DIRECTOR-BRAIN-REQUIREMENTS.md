## 1. Goal
The brain is a CINEMATIC DIRECTOR (and creator): it must think, reason and invent like a film director, with real cinematography knowledge, the way our reference channels do. Viewers should understand the story with the video MUTED.

## 2. Colour: dynamic, invented by reasoning
- Our purple/magenta look is one look. The brain may also go natural colours. It decides, per shot and scene, where a fictional palette fits and where a natural one fits, always in "our type" of colours.
- Keep: existing palettes, colour research, rim light and rim colour. Change: the overall colour adjustment becomes dynamic.
- Brain must reason from what kind of shot it is. A second channel he likes has the same premium level plus a soft glow/bloom in daylight and natural colours.

## 3. Diagrams / drawings
Same drawing and diagram devices as ours, but dynamic: the brain thinks where a diagram is needed and where not.

## 4. Alive, real, 3D-feeling frames
- Background life: e.g. a stranger passing behind in a city, slightly blurred; other things the brain thinks of.
- Foreground life: e.g. a slightly blurred wooden pole in the foreground, present because something wooden is around; the brain invents motivated foreground objects for depth.
- Real lens blur and focus, like high-end cinema cameras: the closer the shot, the stronger the background blur. Strength should feel "real".
- A 3D camera effect in close-ups where it suits.

## 5. Real-life (non-diagram) shots and handheld
- "Second-person" real-life shots with realistic movement in the frame, e.g. a character walking on a road and a car may hit them.
- Handheld camera movement: cinematic, NOTICEABLE (not very subtle), dynamic: its strength follows the action (character running toward the camera = faster, stronger handheld).
- Mostly used on close-ups of real-life moments, not on diagrams.

## 6. Dialogue (not only narration)
- The scripts have narration AND character dialogue. Example: two characters talking, camera on one face, close-up, rule-of-thirds framing, handheld: very professional and cinematic. The director brain must handle dialogue shots; do not design on pure narration.
- Voices: one narrator voice across the channel; different voices per video for the characters (his plan).
- Where dialogue vs narration goes is a scripting-brain job later; the director brain must first be able to do whatever the script gives it.

## 7. Two new library files he asked for
1. CINEMATIC CAMERA ANGLES / SHOTS library: every shot type films use (close-up, thirds framing, top angle, ... all of cinema) so the brain can pick dynamically.
2. CINEMATIC EFFECTS-FOR-FEEL library: e.g. handheld and all the others; the brain reasons about them and invents new ones.

## 8. The brain must also think about
- Transitions: where needed and which look cinematic and premium.
- Where handheld goes; where focus/blur is stronger.
- How characters walk, the physics, everything about movement; real cinematic knowledge.
- Inventing any person the script needs (random stranger, doctor, anything) and any object, in our own style.
- What makes a scene alive and 3D: well-used thirds, people walking past, quality of blur.
- Text: where to add it, where not, what colour, which font suits the feeling of the script.
- Feel first: the picture and motion must carry what the script says.
